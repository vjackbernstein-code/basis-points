No money is invested. These are hypothetical results. Six books run over the same screen, the same prices and the same frictions, differing only in their rules, so that the effect of each rule can be seen rather than assumed. Each starts from a notional $100,000, rebalances weekly (Monday), except book F every 4 weeks, and pays 40 basis points per side; a stop exit pays a further 75 because real stops gap through in thin small-caps. The path column shares one vertical scale across all six rows, so the shapes can be compared directly. Watch the friction column before the return column — this screen turns over its holdings many times a year, and at that rate the cost of trading may be the whole story rather than a rounding error. Each book’s name links to its own page — what it holds, at what size, and where every stop sits. Book A is the control — if the cleverer books do not beat it, the cleverness is not earning its keep. Simulated results still omit what hurts real traders most: the market moving against a real order, taxes, and the nerve to follow a system through a losing stretch.
