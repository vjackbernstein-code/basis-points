The take

Geopolitics is setting the tape. Renewed U.S.–Iran hostilities near the Strait of Hormuz pushed crude back toward $90 intraday (our tile shows the last official end-of-day print), and that oil spike is doing the real damage — not to stock prices, which slipped only modestly, but to the inflation math. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 6 basis points to 4.73%, its highest of the cycle, as markets started to price the once-unthinkable: that the Fed's next move could be a hike rather than a cut. Fed chair Warsh has not pushed back on that read, and a September meeting that was supposed to be quiet suddenly is not.

Nothing tradeable today, but this is how regulatory themes start, and AI-exposed names have been the market's main engine.

What to watch from here: whether the 10-year holds above 4.7%; how quickly the oil move shows up in September inflation expectations; retailers' diverging tariff-refund accounting, which is muddying earnings comparisons across the sector; and crypto, today's single most-covered theme, with bitcoin holding near $79,000.
